# Concept tags — the chain's load-bearing primitive

For BookChain to actually *chain*, chapters need to declare what concepts they teach and what concepts they require. The `chain-index` generator walks these tags across all books in the chain and produces a cross-book concept graph.

## Tag syntax

`<language>/<concept-slug>[/<aspect>]`

- `<language>` is the target language (rust, swift, swiftui, go, ...) or `meta` for pedagogy-level concepts (`meta/predict-before-run`, `meta/pain-anchor-framing`).
- `<concept-slug>` is kebab-case. Stay terse.
- `<aspect>` is optional. Use it when a chapter teaches part of a concept and a later chapter teaches the rest. For example, `rust/drop-trait/gloss` and `rust/drop-trait/full` can name two depths of the same concept.

Examples:
- `rust/cargo-workspaces`
- `rust/tokio-process-spawning`
- `rust/option-take`
- `rust/drop-trait/gloss`
- `swiftui/state-binding`
- `swiftui/view-protocol`
- `meta/predict-before-run`

## Frontmatter fields

Every chapter's frontmatter gains two list-valued fields:

```yaml
teaches:
  - rust/tokio-process-spawning
  - rust/option-take
  - rust/drop-trait/gloss
requires:
  - rust/cargo-workspaces
  - rust/cargo-conventions
```

`teaches:` is what the chapter introduces or deepens. `requires:` is what the chapter assumes the reader has already learned. The chain-index uses `requires:` to compute prerequisites and flag broken chains (a chapter requires a concept no chapter in the chain teaches).

## When to add a tag

Tag a concept when:

- A learner returning to it three months later would need to relearn it without the tag.
- It's a load-bearing idea, not a syntactic detail.
- It might be referenced by a later chapter (in this book or a sibling).

Don't tag:

- Tiny syntactic facts ("`let mut` exists"). Keep frontmatter focused.
- Project-specific decisions (such as an IPC envelope format) belong in chapter prose, not the concept namespace.

A good chapter tags 3–8 concepts in `teaches:` and 0–6 in `requires:`. More than that suggests the chapter is doing too much (rule 3 — one new concept per session) or has too many ambient prerequisites (consider adding a recap section).

## Naming discipline

Concepts that recur across books should share names. `rust/drop-trait` in one book and another book should use the same tag. The chain-index detects accidental variants such as `rust/drop-traits`.

When you're not sure if a concept exists in another book, run `<bookgen-skill-dir>/scripts/chain_index.py --book-repo <repo> --also-list-concepts` to print the current concept namespace, and check the starter namespaces under `references/concept-namespaces/<lang>.md`.

## When concepts split or merge

- **Split**: a chapter that taught `rust/ownership` becomes too dense; you split it into `rust/ownership/move-semantics` and `rust/ownership/borrow-checker`. Update the chapter's `teaches:`. The chain-index will flag any sibling chapters whose `requires: [rust/ownership]` no longer resolves; you decide whether to update them or the namespace.
- **Merge**: two chapters in different books taught what turns out to be the same concept under different names. Pick a canonical name, rename in both, update the chain-index.

Both operations are cheap because the tags are textual and the chain-index regenerates from scratch.

## Pre-baked concept namespaces

The bookgen skill ships starter concept lists per language under `references/concept-namespaces/<lang>.md` (rust, go, swift, swiftui, zig, ocaml, elixir, plus `meta.md` for pedagogy-level tags). New chapters should pull canonical slugs from these where possible to keep the chain coherent across books generated by different agents/learners. The lists are starters, not exhaustive — extend them as the chain grows, and prefer reusing an existing slug over coining a near-duplicate.

## Anti-patterns

- **Tag inflation**: tagging every keyword that appears in the chapter. The tags exist for cross-chapter linking, not as an SEO index. Stay disciplined.
- **Untagged chapters**: a chapter with no `teaches:` field is invisible to the chain. Even ceremony chapters teach *something* (e.g., `meta/build-philosophy`). If a chapter genuinely teaches nothing, it shouldn't be a chapter.
- **`requires:` lists that match the previous chapter exactly**: this is just "the previous chapter," redundant with chapter ordering. Use `requires:` only for the *non-obvious* dependencies, especially cross-book ones.
- **Cross-language tags**: don't reference `rust/drop-trait` from a SwiftUI book chapter unless the chapter explicitly draws the analogy. Cross-language anchoring belongs in the prose; tag what the chapter teaches in *its* language.
